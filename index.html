Why OpenFIM

Food-is-Medicine decisions are rarely simple. An AI system can know many individual nutrition facts and still get the overall decision wrong.

Diet  ·  Health conditions  ·  Laboratory findings  ·  Medications  ·  Food access  ·  Culture  ·  Household circumstances

A useful evaluation may need to weigh all of the above at once. OpenFIM is developing an open way to test that difference, between knowing nutrition facts and making a defensible nutrition decision.

From population data to meaningful AI evaluation

01 — Source

Population data

NHANES and other appropriate sources provide real-world information about people, diets, health, and context.

02 — Construct

Person-level cases

Source data is transformed into structured nutrition decision cases.

03 — Review

Expert judgment

Independent nutrition professionals evaluate what matters, what should be prioritized, and what recommendations are defensible.

04 — Test

AI evaluation

Different AI systems are evaluated using the same cases and transparent criteria.

05 — Improve

Open benchmark

Cases, methods, provenance, evaluation procedures, and lessons can be improved by the broader community.

NHANES is the source data.
OpenFIM is the evaluation layer.

What are we evaluating?

OpenFIM is initially exploring three connected tasks.

Assess

Assess

Can the system identify the information that actually matters?

Prioritize

Prioritize

Can it identify the most important nutrition problem in a complex case?

Intervene

Intervene

Can it propose realistic nutrition changes while accounting for health, safety, resources, and context?

Across all three

Safety matters. We are particularly interested in multimorbidity, competing nutrition priorities, uncertainty, inappropriate recommendations, unsupported therapeutic claims, and failure to recognize when professional care is needed.

Why expert judgment?

Nutrition decisions do not always have one correct answer. Two qualified dietitians may recommend different approaches while both remain professionally defensible.

OpenFIM therefore explores evaluation based not only on a single "gold answer," but on:

Agreement

What experts commonly identify

What experts commonly identify or recommend.

Acceptable variation

Different, defensible approaches

Different approaches that remain defensible.

Disagreement

Where judgment differs

Places where expert judgment genuinely differs.

Safety boundaries

Where recommendations cross the line

Recommendations that cross important professional or clinical boundaries.

This allows us to ask a more useful question: does an AI system stay within the range of professionally defensible nutrition judgment?

Built in the open

OpenFIM-Bench is new and intentionally being developed in public. Today, the repository contains early work on:

  • NHANES extraction and harmonization
  • Population-grounded case construction
  • Complex and multimorbid cases
  • Synthetic/augmented challenge cases
  • Narrative and contextual information
  • Adversarial evaluation

The benchmark specification, expert-review process, governance model, contribution pathways, and long-term stewardship are still being developed.

We are opening the process before those decisions become fixed.

The community we are building

Our goal is a progression from:

Discover→ Try→ Contribute→ Review→ Govern

People should be able to enter OpenFIM through the form of contribution that matches their expertise and, over time, take on deeper roles in reviewing and governing the ecosystem. The benchmark should not be permanently controlled by the people who created it.

Our near-term questions

We are intentionally leaving important questions open.

Q.What cases matter most?
Q.How many independent experts are needed?
Q.How should expert disagreement be represented?
Q.What makes a recommendation unsafe?
Q.Which contextual information is essential?
Q.How should synthetic challenge cases be validated?
Q.What should be community-governed?
Q.What is the right long-term home for OpenFIM?

These are not side questions. They are central to building a credible open evaluation ecosystem.
